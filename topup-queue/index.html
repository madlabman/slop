How to read the result

The model uses the contract’s FIFO top-up order plus the routing assumptions listed below, then explains the result in operator terms.

1

Every seat starts at 32 ETH

One queue item represents one validator. It needs up to another 2,016 ETH to reach the 2,048 ETH maximum effective balance.

2

Top-ups are first-in, first-out

ETH goes to the queue head in 2 ETH increments. The operator's cohort is at the tail, so every earlier queued validator is funded first.

3

Return has two components

Bond shares earn the stETH rebase, while the operator also receives 2% of validator rewards. The rebase dominates early; the fee component grows as the validator is topped up.

Operator economics 32/30 ETH bond · 2% fee · 2.175% bond APR Bond shares participate in the stETH rebase, modeled from Lido's 7-day SMA API. The first key requires 32 ETH and each later key 30 ETH; the lower average bond modestly raises the operator-fee component of APR. Validator performance is fixed at 100%. Following the proposal's capital-efficiency convention, this model compares total operator APR with gross solo validator APR; it is not the raw stake-to-bond ratio. Total operator APR adds bond rebase and fee APR because they are separate returns on the same required bond denominator. Fee-reward compounding after distribution is not modeled.
Queue state Full · operator cohort at tail The starting snapshot has a full queue whose initial 32 ETH deposits predate the 365-day inflow window. Operator keys occupy contiguous positions at the back. If the operator has more keys than the queue can hold, the remaining keys wait outside it and enter one by one as existing validators finish.
This is a deterministic policy model, not a forecast. Top-ups start only after proven CL activation, matching TopUpGateway. Displayed APR blends the operator's keys over the first 365 calendar days from the starting snapshot: bond rebase accrues throughout, while each key's operator fees start at its activation as enforced by the Performance Oracle. Activation waiting therefore reduces realized fee APR. The fixed fully topped benchmark assumes every operator key is deposited at the starting snapshot and has 2,048 ETH when the selected activation wait ends; it therefore captures both seat-wait and top-up drag. The model assumes enough key demand to refill freed seats and a 2,048 ETH Router target. It omits exits, penalties, MEV variance, variable pending-deposit delays, CL effective-balance hysteresis, and competition with other modules. Allocated top-ups become reward-effective one day later in this model. Bond rebase is simple APR. A free seat reserves inflow until it can receive its 32 ETH initial deposit before further top-ups, and a completed seat can refill immediately. Inflow unavailable while the FIFO head is inactive is routed elsewhere and is not available to this module on a later day. Inflow absorbed is allocated ETH divided by total modeled inflow during the 365-day window.
